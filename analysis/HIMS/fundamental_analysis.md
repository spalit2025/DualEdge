### 1. Graham's Margin of Safety

**Earnings Power Value (EPV) Calculation:**

To calculate EPV, I need normalized earnings. HIMS just turned profitable in FY2024 after years of losses, so normalization requires judgment.

- **FY2024 Net Income**: $126.0M (first profitable year)
- **FY2024 Operating Income**: $61.9M
- **FY2024 EBITDA**: $79.0M
- **Stock-Based Compensation (SBC)**: $92.3M in FY2024 — this is a real economic cost that depresses true cash earnings
- **Adjusted Earnings (Net Income - excess SBC impact)**: Using operating income as a more conservative base: $61.9M

However, FY2024 included a large deferred tax benefit ($61.6M in Q3 2024 alone and -$54.3M deferred tax in the cash flow). The tax provision was actually a net negative (benefit) of -$54.3M for the full year. Stripping out this one-time tax benefit, pretax income was $71.7M with a normalized 21% tax rate, yielding ~$56.6M in normalized after-tax earnings.

For a more forward-looking EPV using trailing four quarters (Q4 2024 through Q3 2025):
- Trailing 4Q revenue: $481M + $586M + $545M + $599M = $2.211B
- Trailing 4Q net income: $26.0M + $49.5M + $42.5M + $15.8M = $133.8M
- However, the EPS trajectory is sharply declining: $0.32 -> $0.11 -> $0.20 -> $0.17 -> $0.06

**Using normalized earnings of ~$130M (splitting the difference between FY2024 and trailing 4Q):**

- **Cost of Capital**: 10-year Treasury ~4.2% + 5% equity risk premium = 9.2%
- **EPV = Normalized Earnings / Cost of Capital = $130M / 0.092 = $1.413B**
- **Current Market Cap**: $4.17B
- **Premium to EPV**: Market cap is 2.95x EPV, implying a -66% "negative margin of safety"

Even using the more generous forward EPS ($1.12 x 228M shares = ~$255M forward earnings):
- **Forward EPV = $255M / 0.092 = $2.77B**
- Still a 50% premium to forward EPV

**Verdict**: HIMS fails the Graham test decisively. The current price embeds enormous growth expectations. At $18.33, the market is pricing in earnings power far beyond what the company currently generates. Even the forward estimate leaves a significant gap. However, this is typical for a high-growth company transitioning to profitability — Graham's framework is inherently biased against growth stories.

**Graham Score**: FAIL (market price 2-3x current EPV; would need ~$6-$7 per share for 15% margin of safety on current earnings)

### 2. Buffett's Economic Moat

**Evidence from 10-K and financials:**

**Switching Costs: MODERATE**
- Subscription-based model with recurring billing (30 to 360-day cadences) creates behavioral inertia
- Customers have established medical records, telehealth history, and prescriptions within the platform
- Provider relationships and personalized treatment plans create friction for switching
- However, switching to a competitor telehealth platform or traditional doctor is not technically difficult

**Brand Power: MODERATE-TO-STRONG**
- The 10-K emphasizes brand as a core differentiator, with significant investments in marketing ($679M in selling/marketing in FY2024, 46% of revenue)
- The company has normalized seeking treatment for historically stigmatized conditions (sexual health, hair loss, mental health)
- Celebrity partnerships and retail presence (brick-and-mortar) expand awareness
- Over 30 million telehealth consultations facilitated since founding

**Vertical Integration / Cost Advantages: DEVELOPING**
- Owns pharmacy operations (XeCare in Ohio, Apostrophe Pharmacy in Arizona)
- Acquired MedisourceRx (503B compounding outsourcing facility) in 2024
- Acquired peptide manufacturing (C&S Bio) and lab testing (Trybe Labs) in Feb 2025
- These owned facilities reduce reliance on third-party pharmacies, improving margins
- Gross margin of 79.4% in FY2024 (up from 82% in FY2023 — slight decline, but still very strong for a healthcare company)

**Network Effects: WEAK**
- The platform does not exhibit strong direct network effects (patients don't benefit from more patients)
- Some indirect effects: more patients attract more providers, which improves service quality and speed
- Data advantages accumulate with scale (30M+ consultations provide clinical insights for personalization)

**Regulatory Barriers: MODERATE**
- Complex regulatory environment across 50 states for telehealth, pharmacy, and compounding creates barriers to entry
- Affiliated Medical Group structure required by corporate practice of medicine prohibitions is complex to replicate
- However, regulatory risk cuts both ways — FDA actions on compounded GLP-1s (semaglutide) are a major threat

**Moat Assessment: NARROW**

The moat is real but narrow. Brand recognition + subscription stickiness + vertical integration into pharmacy/compounding create a defensible position. But the moat is not wide because: (1) telehealth competition is fierce (Teladoc, Ro, Noom, etc.), (2) barriers to switching are psychological, not structural, (3) regulatory risk on compounded drugs could remove a significant revenue driver, and (4) the massive marketing spend (46% of revenue) suggests the brand cannot stand on its own without continuous investment.

### 3. Damodaran's Growth Reasonableness

**Implied Growth Rate from Current Valuation:**

- **Trailing P/E**: 34.6x on $0.53 EPS
- **Forward P/E**: 16.4x on $1.12 forward EPS
- The gap between trailing and forward P/E implies the market expects earnings to more than double (from $0.53 to $1.12)

**Historical Revenue CAGR:**
- FY2021 to FY2024 (3-year): ($1.477B / $272M)^(1/3) - 1 = 75.4% CAGR
- FY2022 to FY2024 (2-year): ($1.477B / $527M)^(1/2) - 1 = 67.4% CAGR
- FY2023 to FY2024 (1-year): 69.3% YoY

Trailing 4Q revenue annualized: $2.211B, representing ~50% growth over FY2024's $1.477B. Revenue growth is decelerating but still substantial.

**Reinvestment Rate x ROIC:**
- FY2024 capex: $52.8M, R&D: $78.8M, Total reinvestment (capex + intangibles + acquisitions): ~$79M
- Invested capital (equity + debt): ~$488M
- ROIC = NOPAT / Invested Capital = ($61.9M x (1 - 0.21)) / $488M = ~10%
- Reinvestment rate = (capex + acquisitions) / NOPAT = $79M / $48.9M = ~162% (reinvesting more than they earn — typical for growth phase)
- Sustainable growth = ROIC x reinvestment rate, but this framework breaks down when a company is reinvesting more than it earns

**Growth Reasonableness Assessment:**

The forward P/E of 16.4x is reasonable IF forward estimates hold. However, there is a critical concern: quarterly EPS is deteriorating rapidly (Q3 2024: $0.32, Q4 2024: $0.11, Q1 2025: $0.20, Q2 2025: $0.17, Q3 2025: $0.06). This suggests $1.12 forward EPS may be overly optimistic. Revenue growth remains strong (Q3 2025 $599M vs Q3 2024 $402M = 49% YoY), but operating expenses are growing faster than operating income in recent quarters.

The massive increase in total debt (from $11.3M in Q4 2024 to $1.12B by Q3 2025) indicates significant recent capital raises, likely for acquisitions (peptide manufacturing, lab facility). This debt load materially changes the risk profile.

**Verdict**: OPTIMISTIC. The market appears to be pricing in revenue growth continuation at 30-40% with expanding margins. The EPS trend line and new debt load suggest this is optimistic. Revenue growth is genuine, but the path to sustained profitability is less certain than the forward P/E implies.

### 4. Lynch's PEG Check

**PEG Ratio Calculation:**

- **Trailing P/E**: 34.6x
- **Forward P/E**: 16.4x

**Earnings Growth Rate:**
- FY2023 to FY2024 EPS: -$0.11 to $0.53 — not meaningful (loss to profit transition)
- Revenue growth rate (most recent): ~50% (trailing 4Q vs FY2024), decelerating from ~69%
- Forward earnings growth implied: $0.53 to $1.12 = 111% growth

Using forward consensus growth:
- **PEG (trailing P/E / forward growth)**: 34.6 / 111 = 0.31 (VERY attractive if growth materializes)
- **PEG (forward P/E / revenue growth)**: 16.4 / 50 = 0.33

However, the recent quarterly EPS trajectory is concerning. Using a more conservative normalized growth rate:
- If we assume long-term sustainable earnings growth of 25-30% (as revenue growth decelerates and the company matures):
- **PEG (trailing P/E / 25%)**: 34.6 / 25 = 1.38
- **PEG (trailing P/E / 30%)**: 34.6 / 30 = 1.15

**Verdict**:
- Optimistic PEG (using forward consensus): 0.3 — deeply UNDERVALUED
- Conservative PEG (using sustainable growth): 1.15 - 1.38 — FAIR value
- Risk-neutral threshold: accept up to 2.0 — HIMS passes on all scenarios

**Lynch Classification**: Fair to Undervalued (PEG 0.3 - 1.4 depending on growth assumptions). Even the conservative case is well below the 2.0 threshold for a risk-neutral investor.

### FRAMEWORK SYNTHESIS

**Framework Agreement/Conflict Matrix:**

| Framework | Signal | Conviction |

| Graham EPV | SELL (price 2-3x EPV) | High |
| Buffett Moat | Neutral (Narrow moat, brand + subscription) | Moderate |

| Piotroski F-Score | Lean BUY (6/9, improving fundamentals) | Moderate |

**Where Frameworks Agree:**
- Growth is genuine and exceptional (75% 3-year revenue CAGR)
- The business has positive operating momentum (first profitable year, strong OCF)
- The subscription model creates revenue predictability

**Where Frameworks Conflict:**
- Graham says dramatically overvalued; Lynch says undervalued on PEG basis
- Piotroski shows improving fundamentals, but quarterly EPS is deteriorating
- Analyst price targets suggest 69% upside, but every single insider is selling aggressively

**Key Risk Factors:**
1. **Quarterly EPS deterioration**: Q3 2025 EPS of $0.06 (vs $0.32 a year ago) despite 49% revenue growth suggests margin pressure. Operating expenses are scaling faster than revenue in recent quarters.
2. **Massive debt increase**: From $11M to $1.12B in 9 months (Q4 2024 to Q3 2025). The acquisitions (peptide manufacturing, lab testing) are unproven and add execution risk.
3. **Insider selling**: Universal, sustained, and large-scale. No insider is buying at any price.
4. **Regulatory risk**: Heavy reliance on compounded medications, particularly weight loss (GLP-1 compounding), which faces ongoing FDA scrutiny. A regulatory crackdown could remove a meaningful revenue stream.
5. **Stock sitting at 52-week low**: $18.33 is the 52-week low, down 71.9% from the high of $68.74. Technical indicators (RSI 18.3, Weinstein Stage 4, below Bollinger lower band) all confirm severe downtrend.

**Final Assessment:**

This is a genuinely high-quality business experiencing explosive growth in a large addressable market. The subscription model, vertical integration into pharmacy/compounding, and brand recognition are real competitive advantages. The FY2024 profitability inflection was a meaningful milestone.

However, the stock's 72% decline from its high reflects real concerns: deteriorating quarterly profitability (despite revenue growth), massive new debt, and unanimous insider selling. The market is correct to discount these risks.

For a risk-neutral investor, the current price offers a compelling risk/reward. The mean analyst target of $31 implies 69% upside. The forward P/E of 16.4x is attractive for a company growing revenue at 50%. Even the conservative PEG of 1.4 is reasonable. The Piotroski score of 6/9 confirms the business is fundamentally improving.

The BUY call is made with moderate conviction (6/10) because the insider selling pattern and Q3 2025 EPS collapse create genuine uncertainty. The stock could easily go lower before it recovers. But at 72% off its highs with the business still growing revenue at 50% and generating positive free cash flow, the asymmetry favors the long side for a risk-neutral investor willing to tolerate volatility.

**RECOMMENDATION: BUY | CONFIDENCE: 6/10**
