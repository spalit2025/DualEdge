### 6. Mean Reversion Check

| Metric | Value | Signal |
|--------|-------|--------|
| RSI (14-day) | 18.32 | Deeply Oversold (< 30) |
| Distance from 200-day MA | -57.7% ($19.33 vs $45.75) | Extremely Extended |
| Price Z-Score (6-month) | -2.35 | Extreme (beyond -2.0 threshold) |
| **Extension Assessment** | | Triple oversold extreme |

**Analysis**: The mean reversion indicators are flashing at extreme levels across all three metrics. This is the one area where a contrarian bull could construct an argument.

RSI at 18.32 is deeply oversold. For reference, RSI readings below 20 are rare for any stock and typically occur only during capitulation events or flash crashes. Historically, RSI this low has preceded at least short-term bounces. However, during genuine structural declines (as opposed to temporary dislocations), RSI can remain below 30 for extended periods, and each successive bounce tends to be shallower.

The -57.7% distance from the 200-day MA is extraordinary. Stocks rarely trade this far from their 200-day MA without some mean reversion attempt. But it is critical to recognize that mean reversion works both ways: the 200-day MA itself will eventually fall toward price, not just price reverting to the MA. With the 200-day MA slope classified as "flat" and price plummeting, the MA is about to roll over hard, and the "mean" that price reverts to will be much lower than $45.75 in the coming months.

The Z-score of -2.35 confirms the stock is trading 2.35 standard deviations below its 6-month mean price. In a normal distribution, this level is reached only ~0.9% of the time. While this is statistically extreme, the distribution of stock returns is not normal -- it has fat tails, and stocks in fundamental distress can and do stay at Z-score extremes for weeks or months.

**The critical distinction for the risk-neutral investor**: Mean reversion signals are most reliable in range-bound or uptrending stocks experiencing temporary pullbacks. In a Stage 4 decline with deteriorating fundamentals (EPS falling from $0.32 to $0.06 per quarter), the "mean" is a moving target that is itself declining. Catching a falling knife based on oversold readings alone is a well-documented way to incur losses. The correct interpretation of these extreme readings is that the stock is oversold enough to produce a counter-trend bounce, but a bounce is not a buy signal. The primary trend is down, and oversold conditions in a downtrend are symptomatic of the trend, not a reversal signal.

Concretely: HIMS could bounce 20-30% from these levels (a dead-cat bounce from $19.33 to the $23-25 area is plausible), but the risk of further decline to the analyst low target of $13.00 -- an additional 33% downside -- makes the risk/reward asymmetric to the downside.

**Framework Verdict**: SELL (oversold conditions in a Stage 4 decline are a symptom of the trend, not a reversal signal; potential for a tactical bounce does not override the primary downtrend).
