**Analysis:**

AMZN is classified as Weinstein Stage 3 (Topping) with moderate confidence. The hallmarks are clear: the 30-week moving average has flattened after its prior uptrend, and price has broken decisively below this critical trend anchor. The price-to-MA ratio of 0.9146 indicates the stock is trading nearly 8.5% below its 30-week MA — a significant gap that in Weinstein's framework indicates the topping process is well advanced and potentially transitioning toward Stage 4 (Declining).

The contracting volume pattern is the one ambiguous data point. In classical Weinstein theory, a Stage 3 top typically features high-volume distribution followed by declining volume on rallies. The current contraction could mean either (a) selling pressure is abating temporarily before the next leg down, or (b) institutional sellers have finished distributing. Given the sharpness of the recent decline (from $247 to $208 in roughly four weeks), the former interpretation is more likely — this looks like a brief pause in a distributional process, not a completed correction.

For a risk-neutral investor, Stage 3 is a clear SELL signal. Weinstein's framework explicitly warns: never buy or hold a Stage 3 stock. The only acceptable entry for longs is confirmed Stage 2 (price above a rising 30-week MA with expanding volume on advances). AMZN fails all three conditions: price is below the MA, the MA is flat, and volume is contracting.

**Verdict: SELL** — Stage 3 topping with deteriorating price-to-MA ratio. Risk of Stage 4 transition is elevated.

**Analysis:**

The Wyckoff volume picture is unambiguously bearish. Let me break down the key findings:

**1. OBV vs OBV 20-Day MA:** The current OBV of 685M is a staggering 39% below its 20-day moving average of 1,117M. This gap is enormous and indicates sustained, heavy distribution. In Wyckoff's framework, this level of OBV deterioration means that institutional money is leaving the stock aggressively. The cumulative volume balance has shifted decisively in favor of sellers.

**2. No Bullish Divergence:** If OBV were rising while price fell, that would signal hidden accumulation — smart money buying on weakness. That is not happening here. OBV and price are falling in lockstep, which confirms the downtrend has volume conviction behind it. There is no evidence of accumulation.

**3. Volume Ratio of 0.81:** Average volume on up days is only 81% of average volume on down days over the past 60 trading sessions. This confirms the OBV picture: when AMZN goes up, it does so on lighter volume; when it goes down, volume expands. This is the textbook signature of distribution — institutions selling into strength and the stock declining on heavy volume.

**4. Recent Volume Spikes:** Looking at the daily data, the most telling detail is the volume on the Feb 5-6 decline:
- Feb 5: 103.5M shares traded (price dropped to $222.69)

These volumes are 3-5x the normal daily average, occurring on the two largest down days. This is climactic volume on the sell side — a potential selling climax, but in the context of a Stage 3 topping pattern, it is more likely an acceleration of distribution rather than capitulation marking a bottom.

**Wyckoff Phase Classification: Distribution (Phase C-D)**

The combination of price below the 30-week MA, falling OBV, no divergence, and climactic selling volume is consistent with Wyckoff Distribution Phase C (the "sign of weakness" break below support) or Phase D (the confirmed downtrend). The massive volume on Feb 5-6 could be the "sign of weakness" (SOW) event in Wyckoff terminology.

**Verdict: SELL** — Clear distribution pattern with no evidence of accumulation. Volume confirms the bearish price trend. Climactic selling volume on Feb 5-6 is consistent with Wyckoff SOW rather than selling climax/spring.

### 6. Mean Reversion Check

| Metric | Value | Threshold | Status |
|--------|-------|-----------|--------|
| **RSI (14-day)** | 27.84 | <30 = Oversold | OVERSOLD |
| **Price Z-Score** | -2.34 | |Z| > 2.0 = Extreme | EXTREME NEGATIVE |
| **Distance from 200-Day MA** | -6.5% | |dist| > 20% = Extended | Not Extended |
| **Distance from 50-Day MA** | -10.4% | — | Significant gap |
| **Distance from 52-Week High** | -17.8% | — | Deep correction territory |
| **Distance from 52-Week Low** | +24.7% | — | Well above the annual low |

**Analysis — The Bull Case for Mean Reversion:**

On pure mean-reversion metrics, AMZN flashes strongly oversold:

- RSI at 27.84 is deeply oversold territory. Readings below 30 historically precede bounces in approximately 65-70% of cases for large-cap stocks.
- Z-score of -2.34 means the current price is 2.34 standard deviations below the 6-month mean. Statistically, a Z-score beyond +/-2.0 occurs in only ~2.3% of observations. This is a rare extreme.
- The stock is NOT extended from the 200-day MA (-6.5%), meaning the distance is not so extreme as to suggest a full-blown crash overshoot.

If this were a stock in a Stage 2 uptrend experiencing a pullback, these readings would represent a screaming buy signal — an oversold dip within a confirmed bull trend.

**Why Mean Reversion is Subordinate to Trend Here:**

However, mean reversion signals must be interpreted within the trend context, and the trend context is bearish:

1. **Trend structure is broken.** AMZN is below all major moving averages (50-day $232.88, 150-day $229.20, 200-day $223.33, 30-week $228.20). In a bearish trend, oversold readings can stay oversold longer than mean-reversion models expect. The old floor often becomes the new ceiling.

2. **RSI below 30 in a downtrend means different things.** In a Stage 2 uptrend, RSI <30 is a "buy the dip" signal. In a Stage 3/4 environment, RSI <30 can be a "momentum capitulation" signal — the first of several waves of selling, not the last. RSI can reach 20 or even lower in genuine bear phases.

3. **Z-score extreme without a catalyst.** A -2.34 Z-score screams for mean reversion statistically, but the stock needs a catalyst to reverse. With deteriorating relative strength, distribution volume, and flattening earnings, the fundamental catalyst for a V-shaped recovery is unclear.

4. **Distance from 200-day MA is only -6.5%.** The risk-neutral extended threshold of 20% has not been triggered. The stock has room to fall further before even approaching the kind of oversold extreme that reliably triggers institutional "buying the blood" behavior.

**Mean Reversion Probability Assessment:**

A short-term bounce (3-10 days) to the $220-$225 range is highly probable given the RSI and Z-score extremes. However, a sustained recovery back above the 30-week MA ($228.20) would require a change in the distribution pattern and relative strength trajectory that is not currently in evidence. The mean-reversion setup creates tactical bounce potential but does not negate the intermediate-term bearish trend structure.

**Verdict: NEUTRAL-TO-SELL** — Deeply oversold readings create short-term bounce potential, but the bearish trend structure means dip-buying here is catching a falling knife. For a risk-neutral investor with an intermediate-term horizon, the trend signals dominate the oversold readings.
