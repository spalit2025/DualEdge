### 1. Weinstein Stage Analysis
- **30-Week MA Slope**: Rising
- **Price vs 30-Week MA**: At (ratio 1.0179)
- **Price-to-MA Ratio**: 1.0179
- **Volume Pattern**: Contracting
- **Stage Classification**: Stage 3 (Topping)
- **Confidence**: Low

NVDA is classified as Stage 3 — Topping — with low confidence. The 30-week moving average at $182.15 remains technically rising, but price has converged to it, sitting only 1.8% above. The 50-day SMA at $183.57 is flat, which is a classic early warning: the faster average loses momentum before the slower one rolls over. The volume pattern is contracting on a trend basis, though the most recent week (ending 2026-02-02) printed 1.237 billion shares — the heaviest in the 10-week sample — driven by a violent two-day selloff to $171.88 followed by a sharp $13.53 reversal on February 6. This high-volume whipsaw is characteristic of late Stage 3 distribution punctuated by short-covering rallies.

For a risk-averse investor, Stage 3 is a no-buy zone. Weinstein's framework is explicit: the only appropriate entry is early Stage 2 with volume confirmation. NVDA does not meet this criterion. The rising 30-week MA slope prevents a definitive Stage 4 call, which keeps confidence low, but the price behavior (repeated failures near $190-192, then a sharp drop to $171) is structurally bearish.

| Price Trend | Flat |
| OBV vs Price | Confirming weakness (OBV leading price lower) |
| Volume Ratio (Up/Down Days) | 0.98 |
| **Assessment** | **Distribution** |

The Wyckoff volume analysis is the most concerning framework for NVDA's current setup. OBV at 2.819 billion is running 7.3% below its 20-day moving average of 3.041 billion. The OBV trend is falling while price is flat — this is a classic distribution signature where institutional sellers are offloading shares into a range-bound market.

Reviewing the recent 20-day price action confirms this interpretation: the January 27-29 rally from $186.47 to $192.51 occurred on moderate volume (143-172M), but the subsequent decline from $192.51 to $171.88 over February 2-5 occurred on heavy volume (165-207M). The February 6 reversal to $185.41 did print the highest single-day volume in the sample (230.9M), which could indicate capitulation buying — but one day does not negate a multi-week distribution pattern.

The volume ratio of 0.98 quantifies the imbalance: more shares change hands on down days than up days. While marginal, this confirms net selling pressure. In Wyckoff terms, NVDA appears to be in a distribution phase, with supply overwhelming demand at the $190+ level and rallies being sold into.

### SYNTHESIS AND RECOMMENDATION

**Recommendation: SELL | Confidence: 6/10**

The six-framework analysis converges on a risk-averse SELL with moderate confidence:

| Framework | Signal | Weight |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| Weinstein Stage | Stage 3 Topping — no-buy zone | Bearish |
| CAN SLIM | 2/5 — fails technical screen | Bearish |
| Relative Strength | Deteriorating vs SPX at 1m/3m/6m | Bearish |
| Volatility Regime | Normal — no flag, but compressed bands warn of expansion | Neutral |
| Wyckoff Volume | Distribution — OBV falling, volume ratio < 1.0 | Bearish |
| Mean Reversion | Neutral RSI, normal Z-score — not extended | Neutral |

Four of six frameworks are bearish, two are neutral, and zero are bullish. For a risk-averse profile, this is sufficient to issue a SELL recommendation. The confidence is 6/10 rather than higher because: (a) the Weinstein Stage 3 classification carries low confidence due to the still-rising 30-week MA; (b) the February 6 high-volume reversal from $171.88 to $185.41 could mark a shakeout low rather than continued distribution; and (c) NVDA's fundamental earnings power (66.7% quarterly growth, 50.5% annual CAGR) provides a floor that pure technical analysis cannot fully capture.

**What would change this recommendation:**
1. A sustained weekly close above $195 (above the upper Bollinger Band and prior resistance) on expanding volume, confirming a return to Stage 2 advance.
2. RS vs SPX at the 3-month horizon inflecting above 1.0, signaling renewed relative leadership.
3. OBV reversing to an uptrend and exceeding its 20-day MA, indicating accumulation replacing distribution.
4. CAN SLIM score improving to 4/5 or 5/5 (requires new 52-week highs, volume ratio > 1.0, and RS leadership).

If conditions (1) and (2) are met simultaneously, the recommendation would shift to a risk-averse BUY. Any single condition improving in isolation is insufficient given the weight of evidence.
