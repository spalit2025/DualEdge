### (A) GRAHAM'S MARGIN OF SAFETY

**Methodology**: Calculate Earnings Power Value (EPV) using normalized earnings divided by a 9.5% cost of capital, then compare to current market capitalization. For a risk-neutral profile, a margin of safety of 10-15% is acceptable.

**Normalized Earnings Calculation**:
- FY2025 Net Income: $112.01B
- FY2024 Net Income: $93.74B
- FY2023 Net Income: $97.00B
- FY2022 Net Income: $99.80B
- 4-Year Average Net Income (normalized): ($112.01B + $93.74B + $97.00B + $99.80B) / 4 = **$100.64B**

**Earnings Power Value (EPV)**:
- EPV = Normalized Earnings / Cost of Capital
- EPV = $100.64B / 0.095 = **$1,059.4B**

**Market Cap**: $4,087.79B

**Premium / (Discount) to EPV**: ($4,087.79B - $1,059.4B) / $1,059.4B = **+285.9% premium**

**Trailing P/E**: 35.21x
**Forward P/E**: 29.97x
**Historical Context**: Apple's 5-year average P/E has typically ranged from 25-30x. The current trailing P/E of 35.21x sits well above the upper end of this historical range.

**Graham Assessment**: FAIL. The stock trades at nearly 3.9x its earnings power value. Even using the most recent fiscal year's elevated earnings ($112.01B) rather than the 4-year average, EPV = $112.01B / 0.095 = $1,179.0B, still implying a 247% premium. There is no margin of safety whatsoever. For a risk-neutral investor requiring only 10-15% margin, the stock would need to trade at or below ~$1,059B market cap (roughly $72 per share) to meet the Graham threshold. The current price of $278.12 is radically above intrinsic value on an earnings-power basis.

**Score: 0 / 10** (No margin of safety; extreme premium to EPV)

### (B) BUFFETT'S ECONOMIC MOAT

**Assessment based on 10-K filing analysis:**

**1. Pricing Power: STRONG**
- Gross margin for FY2025: $195.20B / $416.16B = **46.9%**, up from 46.2% in FY2024 and 44.1% in FY2023. Consistently expanding margins indicate real pricing power.
- Apple commands premium pricing across all product categories. The 10-K notes competitors seek to compete through "aggressive pricing and very low cost structures" -- yet Apple maintains premium ASPs.
- Services gross margin is even higher, providing a growing high-margin revenue stream.

**2. Switching Costs: VERY HIGH**
- Apple's integrated ecosystem (iOS, macOS, iPadOS, watchOS, visionOS, tvOS) creates enormous switching costs. The 10-K states the company "designs and develops nearly the entire solution for its products, including the hardware, operating system, numerous software applications and related services."
- iCloud, Apple Music, Apple TV+, Apple Pay, Apple Card, Apple Fitness+, and App Store purchases all lock users into the ecosystem.
- Data migration, app repurchase costs, and loss of cross-device integration create high friction for departing customers.

**3. Network Effects: MODERATE**
- App Store creates a two-sided marketplace: developers build for iOS because of the large user base; users stay for the app ecosystem.
- iMessage, AirDrop, and FaceTime create communication network effects within the Apple ecosystem.
- However, most major third-party apps are cross-platform, somewhat limiting this moat source.

**4. Cost Advantages: MODERATE**
- Massive scale ($416.16B revenue) enables favorable component sourcing and manufacturing terms.
- Custom silicon (Apple Silicon) provides both performance and cost advantages vs. off-the-shelf components.
- However, the 10-K notes reliance on "single or limited sources" for certain components, which constrains bargaining power in some areas.

**5. Brand / Intangible Assets: VERY STRONG**
- One of the most valuable brands globally. The 10-K highlights "corporate reputation" as a principal competitive factor.
- Broad IP portfolio: "thousands of applications around the world" for patents, designs, copyrights, and trademarks.

**Moat Rating: WIDE**

Apple possesses a durable competitive advantage driven by ecosystem lock-in, extraordinary brand loyalty, vertically integrated design, and expanding high-margin services. The steady gross margin expansion from 44.1% to 46.9% over three years confirms pricing power is intact and growing.

### (C) DAMODARAN'S GROWTH REASONABLENESS

**Implied Growth Rate from Current Valuation**:
- Trailing P/E: 35.21x
- Using a simplified reverse-DCF framework: For a P/E of 35x to be justified with a 9.5% cost of equity and a terminal growth of 3%, the company needs to deliver approximately 15-18% annual earnings growth for the next 5-7 years.
- Forward P/E of 29.97x implies the market expects ~17.5% EPS growth in the next year: (Forward EPS $9.28 / Trailing EPS $7.90) - 1 = 17.5%.

**Historical Revenue CAGR**:
- FY2022 Revenue: $394.33B
- FY2023 Revenue: $383.29B
- FY2024 Revenue: $391.04B
- FY2025 Revenue: $416.16B
- 3-Year Revenue CAGR (FY2022 to FY2025): ($416.16B / $394.33B)^(1/3) - 1 = **1.8%**
- FY2024 to FY2025 Revenue Growth: ($416.16B / $391.04B) - 1 = **6.4%**

**Historical EPS Growth**:
- FY2022 Diluted EPS: $6.11
- FY2023 Diluted EPS: $6.13
- FY2024 Diluted EPS: $6.08
- FY2025 Diluted EPS: $7.46
- 3-Year EPS CAGR (FY2022 to FY2025): ($7.46 / $6.11)^(1/3) - 1 = **6.9%**

**Earnings Growth Decomposition**:
- Revenue growth: ~1.8% CAGR
- Margin expansion: Gross margin went from ~43.3% (FY2022) to 46.9% (FY2025), contributing ~1-2% annual earnings uplift
- Share buybacks: Shares outstanding declined from 16.33B (FY2022) to 15.00B (FY2025), a ~2.8% annual reduction, adding ~2.8% to EPS growth
- Combined organic EPS growth driver: ~6-7% annually, consistent with observed 6.9% EPS CAGR

**Assessment: OPTIMISTIC**

The market-implied growth rate of 15-18% annualized earnings growth for 5+ years is roughly 2-3x the historical delivered EPS growth rate of 6.9%. While buybacks and services mix-shift can continue to boost EPS, the revenue base of $416B makes double-digit top-line growth structurally difficult. The most recent quarter (Q1 FY2026, Dec 2025) showed strong results with $143.76B revenue and $2.84 EPS, but this represents seasonal peak performance. The market is pricing in a significant and sustained acceleration that is not yet supported by the historical trajectory.

### (D) LYNCH'S PEG CHECK

**Calculation**:
- Trailing P/E: 35.21
- Forward EPS Growth Rate: (Forward EPS $9.28 - Trailing EPS $7.90) / $7.90 = **17.5%**
- PEG Ratio = 35.21 / 17.5 = **2.01**

**Alternative PEG using historical EPS CAGR**:
- 3-Year EPS CAGR: 6.9%
- PEG = 35.21 / 6.9 = **5.10**

**Risk-Neutral Threshold**: Accept up to PEG of 2.0

**Assessment: MARGINAL FAIL**

Using the forward consensus growth estimate, the PEG of 2.01 sits right at the boundary of the risk-neutral threshold of 2.0 -- essentially a coin flip. However, using the more conservative and realized historical EPS CAGR of 6.9%, the PEG balloons to 5.10, far exceeding any reasonable threshold. The forward estimates bake in significant acceleration that may not materialize. On balance, this is a marginal fail for a risk-neutral investor, as the forward PEG barely exceeds 2.0 and the historical PEG is deeply unfavorable.

### SYNTHESIS & FINAL RECOMMENDATION

**Framework Summary**:

| Framework | Result | Signal |
|-----------|--------|--------|
| (A) Graham Margin of Safety | 286% premium to EPV; P/E 35.2x above historical range | **Strongly Bearish** |
| (B) Buffett Economic Moat | Wide moat: ecosystem, brand, pricing power, switching costs | **Strongly Bullish** |
| (C) Damodaran Growth Reasonableness | Market implies 15-18% growth vs. 6.9% historical EPS CAGR | **Bearish** |
| (D) Lynch PEG | Forward PEG 2.01 (marginal fail); Historical PEG 5.10 | **Bearish** |
| (E) Piotroski F-Score | 8/9 -- excellent fundamental quality | **Bullish** |
| (F) Insider & Analyst | All insider selling, no buys; analyst upside only 5-8% | **Neutral/Mildly Bearish** |

**Qualitative Risk Considerations from 10-K**:
- **Tariff Risk**: The 10-K explicitly flags new U.S. tariffs on imports from China, India, Japan, South Korea, Taiwan, Vietnam, and the EU, with a Section 232 semiconductor investigation underway. Apple's supply chain is heavily concentrated in these regions. This represents a material and unresolved headwind.
- **Competitive Intensity**: The 10-K repeatedly emphasizes aggressive price competition, short product life cycles, and competitors willing to operate at zero or negative margins.
- **Supply Chain Concentration**: Single-source dependency on certain components creates operational fragility.
- **Regulatory Risk**: Global regulatory scrutiny of the App Store and digital markets legislation could pressure Services margins.

**The Core Tension**: Apple is unquestionably a wide-moat business with excellent fundamental quality (F-Score 8/9), expanding margins, and a powerful ecosystem. However, the stock's valuation requires a sustained growth trajectory roughly 2-3x what the company has historically delivered. The 35x trailing P/E prices in near-perfection, leaving no margin of safety and minimal room for disappointment.

For a risk-neutral investor, the question becomes: Does a wide moat alone justify paying 3.5-4x earnings power value with only 5-8% analyst upside remaining? The answer is no. The quality of the business is not in question -- the price paid for that quality is.

**FINAL RECOMMENDATION: SELL**
**CONFIDENCE: 6 / 10**

The confidence level is moderate (6/10) rather than high because: (1) The wide moat and F-Score of 8/9 mean the business itself is fundamentally sound and could outperform expectations; (2) The forward PEG of 2.01 is only marginally above the 2.0 threshold; (3) Apple has historically surprised to the upside on Services growth. However, the weight of valuation evidence -- 286% premium to EPV, optimistic implied growth, no insider buying, and limited analyst upside -- tips the balance toward SELL for a risk-neutral investor.
